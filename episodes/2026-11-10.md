## Case of the day

**Lab Genomics LLC / Leena Dalal — $1.25M lab kickback settlement (announced Nov 6, 2026).**
Fountain Valley, California clinical laboratory and its owner agreed to pay $1.25 million to resolve False Claims Act allegations that they paid value- and volume-based commissions to three independent contractor marketers to induce lab test referrals (alleged conduct Jan 2020–Sep 2022). The government alleges they knew the payments violated the Anti-Kickback Statute and structured invoices to make commissions appear to be flat fees. $625,000 of the payment is restitution. Relators Sunil Wadhwa and Ken Newton receive $225,000 (about 18%). The government had previously resolved similar allegations in the same case against Admera Health and Next Bio-Research Services.

**Quest Diagnostics — $13,014,576 plus interest (announced Nov 6, 2026).**
Secaucus, New Jersey lab paid just over $13 million plus interest to resolve allegations that it offered doctors and health care executives illegal kickbacks in the form of New York Giants suite seats at MetLife Stadium, food and alcohol, VIP training camp visits, draft chalk talks, and family combine days to induce referrals (alleged conduct Jul 2013–Aug 2022). The resulting claims were submitted to Medicare and New York and New Jersey Medicaid. Whistleblowers Gerard Abate, MD, Lori Anderson, and Douglas S. Rabin, MD share $2,472,769. Quest denies the allegations; no determination of liability; the settlement does not rule out a future criminal probe. Quest also signed a five-year corporate integrity agreement with HHS-OIG.

## FCA practice note

**Eleventh Circuit, United States ex rel. Zafirov v. Florida Medical Associates, LLC (Sep 1, 2026):** the court vacated Judge Mizelle's Sep 30, 2024 ruling that the FCA's qui tam provisions violate the Appointments Clause, holding relators do not hold a continuing position and are therefore not officers requiring presidential appointment. It joins the Fifth, Sixth, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits in rejecting Appointments Clause challenges. The court expressly declined to reach the Take Care Clause and Vesting Clause challenges and remanded them to the district court. Practical takeaway: Appointments Clause dismissal motions have now lost in five circuits, but the constitutional fight is not over — watch the remand and a possible Supreme Court petition.

## Tech angle

In both lab cases the fraud was proved by lining up two sets of records and watching them disagree: payment records against referral and claims data. Lab Genomics' invoices said flat fee while payments moved with referral value and volume; Quest's entertainment logs and sponsorship records sat against nine years of referral patterns. The paper trail the companies themselves created is the case — and the kind of pattern data analytics is built to catch.

## Also noted

The SEC whistleblower program is rebounding after its weakest year in five (2025: just over $60M to 48 whistleblowers), per a Miller Shah analysis: an April 2026 award of more than $50M, June awards of about $800K, $7M, and $20M, and a September 29, 2026 Commission order awarding more than $12M to one claimant.
