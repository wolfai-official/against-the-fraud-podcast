## Case of the day

**Two House members introduce a bill that would pay whistleblowers to report insider trading on prediction markets (announced Nov 20, 2026; H.R. 11092).** Representatives Sean Casten of Illinois and Ted Lieu of California introduced the Policing Insider Trading in Prediction Markets Act of 2026 on Friday, November 20, 2026. The bill would create a whistleblower program at the Department of Justice: anyone with original, credible information about suspected fraud on a prediction market platform could report it anonymously through counsel, and if the information leads to a successful enforcement action, receive between 10% and 30% of the monetary sanctions. A covered action must be a federal fraud case involving a prediction market platform with sanctions exceeding $1 million; awards would be paid from the DOJ's Assets Forfeiture Fund, with the Attorney General setting the amount based on the significance of the information and assistance. The bill prohibits employer retaliation, provides an appeal process for denied awards, requires the Attorney General to issue guidance within 180 days of enactment, and requires an annual report to the House and Senate Judiciary committees. It is endorsed by Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington, the Project on Government Oversight, Transparency International U.S., the Institute of Internal Auditors, Americans for Financial Reform, and Public Citizen. The bill is a proposal, not law — it was referred to the House Judiciary Committee and the Committee on Education and Workforce and must advance through Congress. The backdrop: on November 13, 2026, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission stayed Kalshi's TRUMPSAY contract series, including a market on what nicknames President Donald Trump will say before 2027, opening proceedings on whether the contracts are readily susceptible to manipulation; Kalshi must respond by December 11. The CFTC has not found the contracts unlawful. The case behind the order: on August 28, 2026, the CFTC settled charges against Gabriel Perez, a former White House teleprompter operator who generally had access to prepared presidential speeches about an hour before delivery and traded Kalshi contracts on whether specific words would appear, from December 2025 through February 2026. He profited on 39 of 43 contracts across 14 mention markets, once changing his position after the president skipped part of a prepared speech. He was ordered to disgorge $107,539.02 in profits, pay a $65,000 civil penalty, and accept a three-year trading ban; he settled without admitting the findings, and the CFTC said his cooperation earned a substantial penalty discount. Kalshi's own surveillance system flagged the trades and referred them to the CFTC. On November 19, 2026, the Senate passed the bipartisan DEATH BETS Act (Senators John Curtis and Adam Schiff), which would bar CFTC-registered exchanges from listing contracts on terrorism, assassination, war, or a person's death. The House Oversight Committee's inquiry into prediction markets has gathered nearly 1,700 pages of documents and included five bipartisan briefings with the platforms.

## FCA practice note

**The SEC's ninety-day claim deadline is real, narrowly excused, and it has already cost claimants their awards this year.** Under Exchange Act Rule 21F-10(b), a whistleblower must file an award claim within 90 calendar days of the SEC posting a Notice of Covered Action. In Release No. 104614 (January 15, 2026), a claimant filed approximately 18 months after the deadline and argued medical issues were extraordinary circumstances. The Commission denied the claim: the Rule 21F-8(a) exception applies only where the claimant shows the late filing was caused by a circumstance beyond the claimant's control, such as attorney misconduct or a serious illness. Providing the information that led to the enforcement action does not excuse lateness, and reopening the claims process after the deadline would be unfair to competing claimants and unworkable. In Release No. 105185 (April 9, 2026), one claimant filed three months late and was denied for lateness alone; a second claimant filed on time but was denied because the investigation had been opened on another tipster's information nearly a year earlier and the new information did not significantly contribute to the case. Practical points: diary the Notice of Covered Action the day it posts; 90 calendar days, not business days; file as soon as practicable even with a genuine excuse; and remember the calendar matters at both ends — the SEC's fiscal 2026 annual report showed 29 awards reduced for unreasonable delay in reporting the tip in the first place.

## Tech angle

**How prediction-market insider trading gets caught: the exchange's own surveillance, the trade's timing, and the product itself.** Kalshi's surveillance system flagged Perez's account: winning 39 of 43 contracts across 14 markets is a statistical signature, not a lucky streak, in markets that should be close to a coin flip for an outsider. The second layer is timing — trades placed in the hour before speeches, when Perez held the scripts and the public did not, plus a position change after the president deviated from the prepared text. The third layer is the regulator's product test: the CFTC asks whether a contract is "readily susceptible to manipulation," and a contract paying out on words one person will say is different from one on an election or a commodity price, because a handful of people — the speechwriter, the teleprompter operator, the advance team — know the answer in advance. The Casten-Lieu bill would add a fourth layer: a paid human source inside the platform or the government office who can name who held the document and who passed it along. The same logic reaches anyone with advance knowledge of a military operation, a regulatory decision, or a public official's announcement trading on a market tied to that event.
