## Case of the day

**Premier Medical Group, P.C. — $2.8M improper-billing settlement (announced Nov 2, 2026).**
Clarksville, Tennessee multi-physician practice agreed to pay $2.8 million to resolve False Claims Act allegations that, from Nov 1, 2018 through May 31, 2023, it submitted claims to Medicare and TRICARE for services performed by non-physician practitioners (nurse practitioners and physician assistants) using physicians' National Provider Identifier numbers. Premier did not admit liability; the claims are allegations only with no determination of liability, and no corporate integrity agreement was imposed. The settlement resulted from an investigation by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Middle District of Tennessee with assistance from the Defense Criminal Investigative Service (DoD OIG). No relator is named in the announcement — it was a government investigation, not a qui tam case.

## FCA practice note

**DOJ/HHS Healthcare False Claims Act Self-Disclosure Pilot Program (announced Oct 22, 2026).** A six-month pilot giving health care companies a coordinated civil resolution for potential FCA violations previously unknown to the government. Terms: repayment of 1.25–1.6 times the base self-disclosure repayment (single damages), no factual admissions, no corporate integrity agreement, no CMS civil monetary penalties, no enrollment revocation. The multiplier turns on timeliness, duration and scope, senior leadership involvement, financial benefit, and prompt remediation. Complete submissions are due by April 22, 2027; late or partial submissions will not be reviewed. Criminal conduct goes through a separate channel — the NFED/HHS-OIG coordination policy announced Oct 15, 2026. Conduct the government already knows about cannot use the pilot, though cooperation credit under Justice Manual § 4-4.112 remains available.

**Leadership watch:** the pilot was announced by the Civil Division, whose head, Assistant Attorney General Brett Shumate, resigned effective Nov 9, 2026 — the fourth senior DOJ official to step down since the midterm elections. Practitioners should watch whether new leadership keeps the pilot on the same terms and whether the FCA case pace changes during the transition.

## Tech angle

NPI-mismatch fraud is caught through claims-data analytics, not a site visit: (1) volume — one physician's NPI attached to more visits than one person could see in a day; (2) cross-matching — the claim names the physician while the EHR audit log and appointment schedule show which clinician actually saw the patient; (3) payment math — the gap between the 85% Medicare rate for NP/PA services billed under their own NPI and the 100% rate for incident-to billing under a physician's NPI, multiplied across thousands of claims, sizes the loss. The data trail the practice itself created is the case.
