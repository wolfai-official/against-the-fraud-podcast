## Case of the day

**ILC Dover / ILC Astrospace — $13M cyber FCA settlement (announced Nov 3, 2026).**
Defense and aerospace contractors agreed to pay $13 million to resolve False Claims Act allegations over cybersecurity failures on contracts with the Army, the Missile Defense Agency, and NASA (alleged conduct Jan 2018–Oct 2024). Relator Philip Root, the former Director of Cybersecurity, receives $2.35M. Relator's counsel calls it the largest government cybersecurity fraud recovery to date under DOJ's Civil Cyber Fraud program. No breach was alleged — the case was won on paperwork and representations.

**Lockheed Martin — ~$29.5M two-part settlement (announced Nov 3, 2026).**
$22M to the United States under the False Claims Act plus ~$7.5M to more than 1,000 employees under an EEOC settlement. Relator Tim Booher, a former Lockheed vice president, receives $4.4M. DOJ's fourth FCA settlement of its kind under the Civil Rights Fraud Initiative (launched May 2025). Lockheed denies the allegations and admits no liability.

## FCA practice note

November 10, 2026 was the date CMMC Phase II was supposed to take effect. It passes as a non-event: the Department of War suspended Phase II on July 13, 2026, pending a comprehensive review, with no restart date announced. Phase I, DFARS 252.204-7012, and NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 still apply.

## Tech angle

In both cases the fraud was found in the record the technology leaves behind — filed assessment scores, system security plans, certifications, and cloud records — not a sophisticated attack.
